# Task Selection

A **topic** is a theme you and your team are interested in; a **task** is something you can actually build and evaluate. Where [Research Topics](/chapters/exploration/research_topics) asked you to commit to a topic, task selection asks your team to pick a well-defined problem within it, one with a clear input and output, a dataset or domain to work with, and a way to measure success.
This section guides you to narrow a topic into a manageable task, identify gaps in current knowledge, and clearly articulate research ideas.

## Exercise

Work in your project team through the following steps to narrow your committed [topic](/chapters/exploration/research_topics) into a specific, executable task. Your goal is to have a concrete task with research objectives and contributions, as well as data and methods necessary to achieve them.

1. As a team, survey the literature freely within your committed topic (e.g., Google Scholar, ACL Anthology, Papers with Code, arXiv, recent proceedings) until you can confidently answer the items below:
   :::tip
   This is a fast, exploratory pass to commit to a task. A deeper, systematic literature review comes later in [Related Work](/chapters/related_work/literature-review).
   :::
   * What specific tasks are people tackling within this topic, and which one(s) seem most promising for you?
   * What data or domains are commonly used, and which are underexplored or newly available?
   * What methods currently perform best, and where do they still fall short?
   * What would a baseline for your project look like, and is it feasible to experiment within a semester?
2. Based on your survey, select a specific task for your team and describe the types of data and/or domains you plan to focus on (10 min).
3. Outline the primary research objectives of your project, which may include:
   :::tip
   The novelty of your research will be assessed based on these objectives. As you write the Intellectual Merit and Approach of your [Framework](/chapters/introduction/framework) later, make sure each objective is framed as a direct response to a specific challenge you raise there.
   :::
   * Devising an innovative methodology that outperforms previous approaches on specific or relevant datasets.
   * Constructing a novel dataset that facilitates task adaptation to new domains or languages.
   * Conducting a comprehensive analysis of state-of-the-art models, focusing on comparative aspects to gain insights into their strengths and weaknesses.
   * Developing a practical application employing existing resources to advance translational research.
4. Create a concise slide presentation summarizing the above activities (one per group) as a preparation for your [checkpoint presentation](/chapters/getting_started/syllabus#checkpoint-presentations) the following week.
