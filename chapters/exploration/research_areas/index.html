Research Areas

Last updated: August 25, 2026

Before diving into AI research, you need to understand what the major research areas in AI are and how they relate to one another. This section surveys major areas in AI research, grounded in the work of active researchers. Towards the end, you should be able to narrow your interest to one or two areas worth exploring further in Research Topics.

Major Areas​

The 8 areas below are actively represented by AI Faculty in the Department of Computer Science at Emory University, so you will be able to connect each one to real, ongoing research (and potential advisors) right here on campus. It is common for a single faculty member to work across multiple areas, especially now that the boundaries between AI research areas are increasingly blurred.

tip

Broad venues such as AAAI 2026 and IJCAI 2026 publish across many areas and are useful for surveying the field, but they are not themselves a single research specialty.

Machine Learning​

The study of algorithms and models that learn patterns from data, including foundational theory, deep learning architectures, and training methods that underlie most modern AI systems.

  • International Conference on Machine Learning 2026 (ICML)
  • International Conference on Learning Representations 2026 (ICLR)
  • Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems 2026 (NeurIPS)
warning

This area is both a research specialty in its own right (new learning methods, architectures, and theory) and a toolkit used across the other areas. Choose ML only if your focus is the learning method itself, not an application domain that merely uses ML.

Natural Language Processing​

The study of how machines understand, generate, and reason with human language, including large language models, dialogue systems, information extraction, and machine translation.

  • Annual Meeting of the Association for Computational Linguistics 2026 (ACL)
  • Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing 2026 (EMNLP)
  • Annual Conference of the Nations of the Americas Chapter of the ACL 2025 (NAACL)

Computer Vision​

The study of how machines interpret and reason about visual data, including image recognition, generation, video understanding, and 3D reconstruction.

  • Conference on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition 2026 (CVPR)
  • European Conference on Computer Vision 2026 (ECCV)
  • International Conference on Computer Vision 2025 (ICCV)

Data Mining​

The study of discovering patterns, structure, and knowledge from large-scale data, including graph mining, recommendation, and database systems for data-intensive applications.

  • ACM International Conference on Knowledge Discovery and Data Mining 2026 (KDD)
  • ACM International Conference on Information and Knowledge Management 2026 (CIKM)
  • IEEE International Conference on Data Mining 2026 (ICDM)

Information Retrieval​

The study of finding and ranking relevant information from large collections, including search engines, recommender systems, and question answering over documents or the web.

  • ACM SIGIR Conference on Research and Development in Information Retrieval 2026 (SIGIR)
  • ACM International Conference on Web Search and Data Mining 2026 (WSDM)
  • ACM Web Conference 2026 (WWW)

AI for Science​

The application of AI methods to accelerate scientific discovery, including computational biology, molecular modeling, materials science, and other domains where models help generate, test, or interpret scientific hypotheses.

  • Intelligent Systems for Molecular Biology 2026 (ISMB)
  • Research in Computational Molecular Biology 2026 (RECOMB)

AI for Systems​

The use of AI to design, manage, and optimize computing systems themselves, including distributed systems, cloud and edge infrastructure, scheduling, and energy-efficient resource management.

  • Conference on Machine Learning and Systems 2026 (MLSys)
  • ACM International Conference on Architectural Support for Programming Languages and Operating Systems 2026 (ASPLOS)

Human-Computer Interaction​

The study of how people interact with computing systems, including interface design, usability, and how humans interact with and are affected by AI systems.

  • ACM CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems 2026 (CHI)
  • Proceedings of the ACM on Interactive, Mobile, Wearable and Ubiquitous Technologies (IMWUT)
  • ACM Symposium on User Interface Software and Technology 2026 (UIST)
info

HCI is not always classified as a core AI area, but AI increasingly shapes how people interact with systems (e.g., conversational agents, explainable AI, human-AI collaboration), making it a relevant adjacent field for AI researchers.

Exercise​

  1. Navigate to [People] in Canvas, type "Research Areas" in the search bar, and form a team of three members. (3 min)
  2. As a team, select three faculty members from AI Faculty whose research sounds interesting, and assign one faculty member to each team member. (5 min)
  3. For your assigned faculty member, identify: which area(s) of AI their research falls under, 1-2 recent key projects or publications, and what problem their work currently focuses on. (10 min)
  4. For each team member, develop two potential research ideas that align with your assigned faculty member's research areas. Share your findings and ideas with your team. (10 min)
  5. As a team, create a concise slide presentation summarizing ideas from steps 3-4, and submit it to [Assignments → Exercises → Research Areas] in Canvas. (10 min)
  6. Prepare to deliver a short (5-minute) presentation of your slides in class.
